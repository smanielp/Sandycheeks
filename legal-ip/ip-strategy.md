## Filing timing

**File provisionals before any public demo, waitlist launch, or press coverage.**

The US has a 12-month grace period from public disclosure for US filings. Other
jurisdictions (PCT, EPO) do not — public disclosure before filing is an absolute
bar to patenting in many markets.

A provisional application establishes a priority date without requiring a full
utility patent specification. Cost: $1,500–4,000 for attorney-drafted provisional.
Cost of missing the window: loss of patent rights in international markets.

**The checklist:**
- [ ] Is the waitlist landing page live? → File provisionals before it goes up
- [ ] Is the demo video public? → File provisionals before it's posted
- [ ] Any press or press inquiries? → File provisionals first

## Patent candidates (from product spec)

### Design patents — form factor

Design patents protect the ornamental appearance of a product. Relevant once the
product form is defined (not yet). Cost: $1,000–2,500. Duration: 15 years from grant.

**What to protect (when design is locked):**
- Overall wagon silhouette and handle
- Battery pack housing appearance
- Handle control interface

### Utility patents — load-assist control method

The e-bike-style load/torque-sensing assist is potentially novel as applied to a
wagon (vs. a bicycle, which is well-covered). A utility patent here would cover
the method of sensing pull force at a wagon handle and proportionally driving a
motor to assist the puller.

**Prior art to search before filing:**
- E-bike torque sensing patents (Bosch, Shimano, Yamaha) — distinguish how
  wagon handle sensing differs from bicycle crank sensing
- Powered utility cart patents — search USPTO for "powered wagon," "electric
  cart assist"

### Utility patents — track drive

The tracked Model 2 variant, if it has a novel drivetrain configuration for the
beach environment. Lower priority than the control method patent; protect after
the wheeled product is de-risked.

## Trade secret considerations

Not everything needs to be patented. Some IP is better protected as a trade secret
(kept confidential, not disclosed in a patent, which becomes public). Candidates:

- Specific corrosion treatment formulation or process (if developed in-house)
- Control tuning parameters (if these create a meaningfully better feel than
  could be reverse-engineered from a product teardown)
- Manufacturing process details that are hard to infer from the finished product

**Note:** a manufacturing partner will need to know manufacturing details.
Use an NDA and a well-drafted manufacturing agreement to protect trade secrets
shared with partners.

## IP in the manufacturing relationship

The manufacturing agreement with any partner must address:
- Who owns tooling? (You do, even if they store it)
- Is the partner prohibited from using your design for a competing product?
- Do you retain all IP developed during the partnership? (Yes — specify it)
- What are the partner's confidentiality obligations?

**Get an attorney to draft the manufacturing agreement before sharing any
design files with a manufacturer.**
