# Concept & Business Brief

## The problem

Soft, dry sand is brutal on wheeled loads. A loaded beach wagon (coolers, gear,
chairs, kids' stuff, fishing tackle) can require near-maximum human effort to
move — especially on the dry sand at the top of the beach, on slopes, and over
ruts. The result: people under-pack, stop short of the water, or abandon the
wagon where it gets stuck. The effort scales with load and distance, which is
exactly when people most want the gear.

## The product

A **powered beach wagon** with load-assist drive purpose-built for soft sand and
the marine environment.

- **Model 1 — Wheeled, powered.** Wide low-PSI balloon tires, hub or geared
  drive motor, removable battery, marine-grade corrosion package. The volume
  product.
- **Model 2 — Tracked, powered.** Rubber-track drive for maximum soft-sand
  traction. Heavier, more complex, more expensive. A halo / second model that
  defines the high end and earns press. Not the launch product.

### The core UX: assist the puller, don't replace them

The winning interaction is **e-bike-style assist** — the motor amplifies the
effort of the person pulling (load/throttle-sensing), rather than a separate
walk-behind joystick that turns the user into an operator. This keeps the
product feeling like *a better wagon*, not a vehicle. "Pedal-assist for your
wagon" is the entire product story in one phrase.

Manual fallback matters: a dead battery must not make the wagon miserable to
pull. Weight discipline is a hard design constraint, not a nice-to-have.

## Positioning

**The marine-grade, premium powered wagon — the e-bike of beach carts.**
Brand-led and design-led, because the underlying tech is not defensible on its
own (founder's own assessment, and correct). Differentiation comes from:

1. Corrosion/ingress engineering that survives salt + sand (the real moat)
2. Load-assist control UX that feels like a wagon, not a machine
3. Brand, design, and distribution
4. Design patents (form factor) + utility patents (load-assist control,
   track-drive) as speed bumps against the inevitable clones

## Personas

**Beachhead — the surf/beach angler.** Hauls 100+ lbs of coolers, rods, sand
spikes, and bait long distances over soft sand, repeatedly. Already spends on
gear, has no dignity hangup about a powered tool, and is reachable through
identifiable communities (forums, tournaments, tackle shops). High intent, low
acquisition cost. **This is the wedge.**

**Scale — the affluent beach/coastal homeowner & family.** Buys for convenience,
lifestyle, and not throwing out their back. Higher volume and margin, but harder
and more expensive to reach, and more design/brand sensitive. **This is where
the business gets big**, entered after the beachhead is won.

**Adjacencies (later, not launch):** boaters/dock owners, campgrounds,
photographers/event gear haulers, overlanders, and a mobility/independence angle
that connects to aging-in-place. Park these; don't dilute the launch.

## Business model options

| Path | What it is | Pros | Cons |
|------|-----------|------|------|
| **A. Retrofit motor kit** | Drive + battery kit that mounts to popular existing beach wagons | Cheapest MVP, fastest demand validation, low tooling | Lower margin, dependent on host carts, harder warranty story |
| **B. Integrator** | Source a proven utility-wagon platform, add motor/wheels/corrosion package, premium brand, sell DTC | Fast to market, tests demand on a real product, moderate capital | Less differentiated, exposed to platform supplier |
| **C. Ground-up product** | Design and tool a purpose-built wagon | Maximum differentiation and IP, real moat | Slow, capital-heavy, riskiest |

**Recommended path:** validate with **A or B**, earn the right to do **C**.
Don't start with C. The first dollar should buy a demand signal, not tooling.

## Revenue & go-to-market (first pass)

- **DTC-first** (own the margin, the customer, and the brand story), with
  beach-town and marine/tackle retail as a second channel once proven.
- Likely premium price point ($800–$2,000+ depending on model) — to be
  validated against COGS and willingness-to-pay in discovery.
- Attachments/accessories and replaceable batteries as margin add-ons.

## The honest risks

- **Clones.** Low conceptual barrier; the founder is right. Moat = brand +
  distribution + corrosion engineering + IP. Plan for cheap Amazon/Alibaba
  copies appearing within a year of proof.
- **Liability & support.** Batteries near water; warranty/returns on a heavy
  object; saltwater corrosion claims. Build the corrosion spec and warranty
  terms early.
- **Capital intensity.** Hardware burns cash on inventory and tooling. Stage
  spend behind validation gates.
- **Weight creep.** Every gram of motor/battery hurts the manual fallback and
  shipping cost. Discipline required.
