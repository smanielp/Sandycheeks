### Discovery — Do These Show Up in Standard Beach Wagon Searches?

**No.** This is a significant finding.

CNN Underscored's 2026 "best beach wagons" roundup features MacSports and Business & Pleasure Co. — both manual. OuterBanks.com features the non-electric Big Kahuna. Field Magazine, Good Housekeeping-adjacent editorial content — all manual. **None of the electric competitors appear in mainstream editorial roundups for "beach wagon."**

The electric beach wagon category is essentially invisible to the mainstream discovery path. A customer searching "best beach wagon" or "beach cart" in Google does not encounter any of these products. They have to already know to search "electric beach wagon" to find them.

This has two implications:
1. **Discovery is the bottleneck**, not the product — the category needs to be created in the customer's mind before they shop it.
2. **Physical retail (like Big Kahuna's Bass Pro placement) is disproportionately valuable** — it's the only way to reach a customer who didn't know to look.

### The 1-Star Pattern
Reported failure modes across the category, from public reviews and complaint records:

- Battery dead after 1 season — salt and moisture ingress into the pack
- Wheels sink in deep soft sand — inadequate balloon tire volume or PSI calibration
- Corrosion: flaking paint, seized axles, weakened frames within months of coastal use
- Difficult or broken folding mechanisms after sand intrusion
- Poor or absent customer service when things fail

> Most units on the market lack IPX4+ waterproofing anywhere near salt spray. "Most standard wagons start showing corrosion within months of regular coastal use."

### The Core Gap
No competitor is leading with **marine-grade durability as a core brand promise**. Corrosion and ingress protection are treated as an afterthought, not a design foundation. This is the unresolved problem across the entire category.

## 2. Patent Landscape

| Patent | Title | Date | Key Claims |
|--------|-------|------|------------|
| US7762363 | Motorized Beach Wagon | 2010 | DC motor drives rear wheels; optional solar charging |
| US8453771 | Beach Wagon | 2013 | 12V battery, dual DC high-torque motors, gearboxes to rear wheels |
| US9908570 | Electric Beach Wagon | 2018 | Motor + gearbox on rear axle, on-board power, throttle lever on handle |
| US20220089207 | Powered Beach Wagon | ~2022 (application) | Motor + battery + actuator + electronic controls tied to a handheld/smart device |

### What's Covered
The **basic powered-wheel architecture** (motor → gearbox → rear axle, throttle on handle) is well-patented from 2010–2018. Standard approaches are not available for clean filing.

### What's Open
- **Load-proportional / torque-sensing assist** — amplifying the puller rather than replacing them. No existing patent covers this control philosophy.
- **Marine-grade sealing architecture** — how to keep salt, sand, and moisture out of the drivetrain and electronics. Not a prominent claim in any filing found.
- **Dead-man / safety UX** — auto-off on release, as a safety feature rather than a mode.
- **Assist ramp profile** — preventing lurch, matching the feel of effort amplification.

These represent the strongest areas for provisional filing before any public prototype showing.

### Recommended Action
File a provisional patent application before any public demonstration of the prototype. The control system and sealing architecture are the most defensible claims.

## 3. Adjacent Category: Golf Caddies

### Market State (2026)
The electric golf caddy market is mature and instructive:

- Lithium-ion batteries are the standard; lead-acid is gone
- Dual brushless motors are baseline for the mid-tier and above
- 36-hole range (~4–6 miles) is the expected minimum
- **Control evolution**: manual throttle → wireless remote handset → Bluetooth "follow me" (the current premium differentiator)
- **Price range**: $400 (basic) to $2,000+ (follow-me models)
- Leading brands: Motocaddy M7, Stewart Q Follow, BATCADDY, Alphard Golf, QOD Golf

### What This Means for Sandycheeks

**The supply chain is yours to use.** Golf caddy components — brushless motors, LiFePO₄ packs, sealed motor controllers, gearboxes — are mature, cost-competitive, and available off the shelf. No need to develop these from scratch.

**No golf caddy brand has crossed into beach.** Golf caddies are optimized for groomed turf; their wheel and traction geometry is irrelevant to soft sand. But the drivetrain and battery components transfer directly.

**The "follow me" model is a v2 / premium tier concept.** Hands-free to the water, with gear arriving automatically. Interesting for a second product tier after the core product is proven.

---

## Strategic Implications

1. **The market exists and has active competition** — not a greenfield pioneer situation. But it's early-stage with clear, recurring failures no one has solved well.

2. **Corrosion is the design moat** — every competitor fails here. A product designed marine-grade from the ground up, with that as the brand promise, occupies unclaimed territory.

3. **The assist philosophy is unoccupied** — all current products replace human effort entirely (throttle-driven). A torque-sensing assist that amplifies the puller is novel UX and likely patentable.

4. **Patent prior art is real but narrow** — don't file on the basic motor-to-axle architecture. Do file on the control system, assist philosophy, and sealing architecture.

5. **Golf caddy supply chain reduces development risk** — source motors, controllers, and packs from that ecosystem; don't design from scratch.
